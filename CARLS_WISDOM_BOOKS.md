## The Joint Book

**Terrie Noll — The Joint Book: The Complete Guide to Wood Joinery.** Chartwell Books; ISBN **9780785822271**. Carl especially likes the internal spiral binding because it stays open on a bench.

The publisher lists edge joints, mortise and tenon, dovetails, dowels and biscuits, hardware, hand and power methods, and jigs. One especially relevant topic in its description is:

> “How the nature of wood and its properties affect joinery, glue, and furniture design.”

— [Quarto / Chartwell Books, The Joint Book](https://www.quarto.com/books/9780785822271/The-Joint-Book).

**Read for our table:** start with accuracy and joint design, then mortise and tenon for the leg/apron corners and edge joining for the top. Compare why a joint is appropriate before copying its cutting method. Dovetails are optional background now that our drawer is omitted.

Carl's [original Porter Square Books listing](https://portersquarebooks.com/book/9780785822271) could not be read during this check. The publisher page confirms the title, author, ISBN, and format; it does not confirm shelf stock at the bookstore.

## The Complete Manual of Woodworking

**Albert Jackson and David Day — The Complete Manual of Woodworking: A Detailed Guide to Design, Techniques, and Tools for the Beginner and Expert.** Knopf paperback; ISBN **9780679766117**. Carl describes it as an older, broader reference rather than a small bench handbook. [Library records](https://search.worldcat.org/title/The-complete-manual-of-woodworking/oclc/593181025) also credit Simon Jennings; the current publisher listing names Jackson and Day.

The publisher describes coverage of woods, tools, and techniques, including:

> “how to choose and use hand tools”

— [Penguin Random House / Knopf, The Complete Manual of Woodworking](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/85845/the-complete-manual-of-woodworking-by-albert-jackson/).

**Read for our table:** stock selection, marking and measuring, sharpening and hand-plane setup, jointing and thicknessing, edge glue-ups, mortise and tenon, assembly, and finishing. Use the actual index in your edition to find those subjects. The point is to understand why a process works before class demonstrates it on our parts.

Carl's [original bookstore listing](https://www.portersquarebooks.com/book/9780679766117) was also blocked to this check. Follow the publisher link for bibliographic details and ask a bookstore or library about availability.

## The Wood Handbook

**Forest Products Laboratory — Wood Handbook: Wood as an Engineering Material, revised 2021, FPL-GTR-282.** Carl points to this publicly funded reference for readers who want the underlying material science. It is a reference to consult by question, not a book a beginner must finish before making a table.

For our build, the official [chapter index](https://research.fs.usda.gov/fpl/wood-handbook) identifies these useful sections:

| Chapter | Question it helps answer |
|---|---|
| 2 — Commercially important woods | What species are we actually buying? |
| 4 — Moisture relations and physical properties | Why does the top need room to move across the grain? |
| 6 — Commercial lumber | How do lumber products and trade descriptions differ? |
| 8 — Fastenings | What matters when selecting and using screws? |
| 10 — Wood adhesives | What makes a sound glued joint? |
| 13 — Drying and dimensional changes | Why can lumber move after purchase and milling? |
| 16 — Finishing wood | What can a finish do, and what remains a material property? |

Read or download [the full publication](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/62200), or begin with [Chapter 4](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/62243). Our [Wood and Buying page](CARLS_WISDOM_WOOD.md#storage-and-seasonal-movement) connects its moisture discussion to the tabletop attachment.

## Magazines, libraries, and a local bookstore

Carl values **Fine Woodworking / Taunton** books and articles as a rich body of instruction, while expressing uncertainty about changes in ownership. Treat that as his dated editorial opinion. Use [Fine Woodworking](https://www.finewoodworking.com/) to look for instruction on a specific problem; some material may require a subscription.

His local bookstore recommendation is [Porter Square Books](https://portersquarebooks.com/). Its homepage is accessible through web browsing, although the two old title pages could not be verified. Bring the ISBN if asking about a title. This collection does not claim current prices, print status, or stock.

Carl also points to the [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/) and [Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/) for older books and historical furniture. His examples include Geoffrey Killen's [Egyptian Woodworking and Furniture](https://archive.org/details/egyptian-woodworking/page/7/mode/2up) and the museum item [Hatnefer's Chair](https://archive.org/details/mma_hatnefers_chair_543868). Access and borrowing restrictions vary by item. These are ways to broaden your eye, rather than substitutes for our dimensions or current shop instruction.

## A reading order for our table

1. Revisit Schwarz's [cut list and drawing](https://www.popularwoodworking.com/app/uploads/2010/10/HiRes-SEPT2004-Seg2.pdf#page=3) beside our [current cutting plan](CUTTING_DIAGRAM.md).
2. Use Noll to understand the apron joints and tabletop edge joints.
3. Use the manual to prepare questions about sharpening, planing, and accurate stock preparation.
4. Consult the Wood Handbook when movement, adhesives, or fastenings raise a question.
5. Explore the [wider reading](CARLS_WISDOM_BEYOND.md) when you want to step away from the cutting list.
