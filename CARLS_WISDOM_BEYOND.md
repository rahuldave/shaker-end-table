## Reverie and everyday attention

Carl playfully assigns ten minutes of daily reverie: time away from digital devices, with no demand to achieve anything. He pairs it with noticing trees, greeting people, helping strangers, picking up litter, and looking at the night sky through the interests represented by [DarkSky International](https://darksky.org/).

> “Eat. Drink. Sleep. Read. Ponder Wood. Ponder Trees. Repeat.”

— Carl Spagnoli, class email, PDF pp. 13–14.

The practical connection is patience. Woodworking takes repeated observation and revision, and the workshop is a shared place. His humor about homework and quizzes is part of that encouragement, rather than a new course requirement created by this website.

## Makers and learning by doing

Carl connects woodworking to the maker movement: experimenting, sharing knowledge, and learning through making. Start with [Make:](https://makezine.com/) for projects and [ISTE's maker-learning essay](https://iste.org/blog/the-maker-movement-a-learning-revolution) for the education discussion. The [Maker Freedom overview](https://makerfreedom.com/what-is-the-maker-movement-5-things-you-should-know/) and [P2P Foundation's index](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Maker_Movement) are additional routes from his email.

For our table, jig-making is an immediate example: make a repeatable way of holding and locating a part, test it, and learn from what happens. Carl's point about experimentation belongs alongside supervision and controlled setups, especially at machines.

He also links Lawrence Lessig's [The Future of Ideas](https://lessig.org/product/the-future-of-ideas/) and [Peter K. Yu's publications](http://www.peteryu.com/publications.htm) while discussing invention, ownership, and access. These are wider intellectual-property reading, not woodworking procedures.

Carl's closing historical and political reflections also mention Adam Smith's *The Wealth of Nations*, Marx's *Das Kapital*, and Thomas Piketty's *Capital in the Twenty-First Century*. They are broader reading prompts, rather than woodworking recommendations. His reference to an 1859 “Origin of Life” appears to mean Darwin's *On the Origin of Species*; that is an editorial inference, not the title as written in the email.

The letter's personal political commentary remains Carl's dated viewpoint. It does not alter the table's dimensions or the class's shop practice.

## Movement, proportion, and systems

Carl suggests dancing as a way to think about balance, timing, body position, and feedback. His local leads are [Boston Swing Central](https://www.bostonswingcentral.org/), [New England Folk Festival Association](https://www.neffa.org/), and [SalsaBoston](http://salsaboston.com/). The websites loaded during the check; no current event dates are reproduced here.

He detours into the golden ratio, Fibonacci, and the square root of two. For our **square 18 x 18-in top**, those are interesting design background, not reasons to replace the recorded dimensions. He also points to [systems theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_theory) and a [Northwestern resource on Cage and Cunningham](https://sites.northwestern.edu/cageanddance/jcmc-late/) while discussing complexity and chance. Chance can inspire composition; machine setups need deliberate control.
